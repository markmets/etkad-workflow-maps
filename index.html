ETKAD Workflow Maps

Can we do for research workflows what Open Syllabus Galaxy did for reading lists? Three attempts, each changing what a dot represents — a tool, a method, a workflow. None supersedes the others: each has a different node, a different corpus, and answers a different question.

The three maps

Workflow Galaxy

Compares whole workflows — the unit ETKAD actually publishes. You choose what “similar” means: same methods, same software, same subject, or same shape (the order steps happen in).

a workflow

which workflows resemble each other, and along which of seven aspects

Method Galaxy

Maps the methods used across European digital humanities, with ETKAD's 13 workflows plotted inside that larger map — the only way to place thirteen workflows on a map at all.

Dot
a method (or, in a second view, an object — see below)
Data
6,305 SSH Open Marketplace items, 2,366 of them tagged with a method, plus ETKAD's 13 workflows joined in through a hand-built Estonian–English TaDiRAH bridge
Shows
which methods are used together across Europe, and which of the 147 methods ETKAD's workflows never touch (74 of 147 — ETKAD uses the other 73)

Tool Galaxy

The prototype: tests whether the underlying technique — shared use becomes map proximity — works at all, on a field with abundant machine-readable data. No humanities content.

Dot
a software tool
Data
1,138 bioinformatics workflows, reduced to 632 distinct tools
Shows
which tools are used together — recovers RNA-seq, genome assembly and proteomics as clusters without being told they exist

Method and object, defined

Method
One research activity from TaDiRAH (Taxonomy of Digital Research Activities in the Humanities) — the controlled vocabulary both the Marketplace and ETKAD use to tag what a piece of work does. Examples: Text Analysis, OCR, Data Mining, Enrichment. 147 distinct methods appear across the corpus Method Galaxy is built from.
Object
Anything in the SSH Open Marketplace that carries at least one method tag: a tool, a piece of training material, a dataset, a publication, or a workflow. 2,366 Marketplace items are tagged objects, plus ETKAD's 13 workflows once joined in — 2,379 in total. An object is usually tagged with one to three methods; ETKAD's workflows carry 8–22 each, because a workflow is several steps, not one action.
Two views
Method Galaxy can show either space. As methods, a dot is one of the 147 activities, and two methods sit close together when the same objects use both. As objects, a dot is one tool, dataset, publication or workflow, and two objects sit close together when they share methods.

How the three relate

A workflow is carried out as a sequence of steps. Each step is usually describable by one or more methods (the TaDiRAH vocabulary), and a method is sometimes carried out with a particular tool. That gives a top-to-bottom relationship: Workflow Galaxy (whole workflows) sits above Method Galaxy (the vocabulary of steps), which sits above Tool Galaxy (the software layer) — a hierarchy of what a project does, not three unrelated maps.

Method Galaxy and Workflow Galaxy share real data: the SSH Open Marketplace, and the same 13 ETKAD workflows. They differ in grain. Method Galaxy indexes every kind of tagged Marketplace object — tools, training material, datasets, publications, workflows — 2,366 of them. Workflow Galaxy only looks at the 108 Marketplace items structured as ordered, step-by-step workflows, and even then it does not compare by method alone: shared methods is one of seven features it weighs, alongside shared software, subject, and step order.

Tool Galaxy shares no data with either. It comes from a separate field — bioinformatics pipelines from WorkflowHub, Dockstore and nf-core — because ETKAD's own workflows don't yet have the per-step, machine-readable tool records that life-science pipelines do. It was built first, to prove the underlying technique works, before trying it on humanities data. The connection worth knowing: Workflow Galaxy already matches a gazetteer of Marketplace tool names against workflow text to spot software mentions — the same kind of tool vocabulary Tool Galaxy is built from, just not yet joined to it.
